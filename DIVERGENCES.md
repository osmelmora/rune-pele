# Divergences from Pele in product mode

Parity mode (`pele_core::render`, `pele parity`) is byte-identical to Pele 1.13.0 and has no divergences.

Product mode (`rune_pele::Diagram::view`) draws the same Scene natively in Rune. Each deliberate difference from Pele's look is listed here with its reason. A difference that is not listed is a bug.

Every one of Pele 1.13.0's 33 types is drawn this way: `rune_pele::lower::NATIVE` lists them all. A source that the last table lists is drawn by Rune's own `rune-mermaid` instead.

## What is drawn differently

| Area | Pele | rune-pele product mode | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Label ink on series colours | Contrasting ink only for series a source overrides. Otherwise labels inherit the background colour, so a 17% label on orange is white. | Ink contrasts with the resolved series colour for every series. | Readability on any palette, including app themes Pele never sees. |
| Fonts | Arial metrics in parity mode, the browser's font in a page. | The app's Rune fonts, measured by Rune at device scale. Monospace text is in the mono family the app gives its `FontCatalog`. | Labels fit the font that is actually drawn. |
| Bold, heading and title weights | `font-weight: bold` (700), and theme weights for a heading inside a node (600) and for a title. | Weight 700 requested for all three. Rune's embedded Inter tops out at SemiBold. | Rune's font set, and the Scene's walk reports one weight. Unverified on other fonts. |
| Sub/superscript shift | `baseline-shift` sub/super. | Smaller runs shifted by 0.3 and −0.45 of the smaller size. | Rune has no baseline-shift. The values are guesses until a type with sub/sup is checked side by side. |
| Series colours a source sets in its theme config | The colours of `themeVariables` such as `git0`, `cScale0`, `pie1` or `xyChart.plotColorPalette` are declared on the SVG's root, so they win over any `--pele-series-*` the page sets. | With `Palette::pele()`, the source's colours, as in Pele. With any other palette, including the window's Rune theme when none is given, the palette's own series, and the source's are not read. Colours a source writes into the diagram itself, such as `style` and `classDef`, win in every palette. | An app's palette is the app's look, so its diagrams agree with one another. `Palette::pele()` is the look the side-by-side holds to Pele's SVG. |
| Default colours | Its own greys, as the fallback of each `--pele-*` variable. | With no palette given, the window's Rune theme: nodes are filled as cards, groups a step from that towards the text, edges and text in the text colour, borders and muted text in the secondary text colour. `Palette::pele()` gives Pele's own. | A diagram in an app looks like the app, in light and dark. |
| Outline behind a title an edge runs through | The title's glyphs stroked 5 wide with round joins, in the colour behind them. | Twelve copies of the title in that colour, set round a circle 2.5 across, under the title. | Rune strokes no glyph outlines. The outline is within a tenth of a point of the stroked one. |
| Hairlines at one device pixel a point | A browser's coverage of a 1 point stroke, by the area of each pixel it covers. resvg, the reference the tests compare with, samples coverage in quarter pixels, and at 1× draws a stroke one device pixel wide or thinner as a hairline with no joins, so a mitred corner has no tip. | Rune's coverage, by each pixel's distance from the stroke's centre line. A slanted or curved hairline is up to a tenth of full coverage darker along its middle. Upright and level ones, and everything at 2×, are the same. | Rune's renderer. Measured over the corpus: `side-by-side.txt` lists the sources where it shows. |
| Curved strokes | One stroke along the curve. | A segment for each stretch of the curve that stays within a quarter of a device pixel of it, each antialiased on its own. Where two meet, a pixel the edge half covers is up to a quarter of full coverage darker. | Rune's `Shape` strokes polylines. Rectangles and circles are Rune boxes instead, which have no joints. |
| Subpaths of one stroke that meet | One stroke over all of a path's subpaths, so where two meet or run along each other, as a railroad branch leaves its track, each pixel is covered once. | Each subpath is stroked and antialiased on its own. At 1× a pixel two of them half cover is darker, by up to a third of full coverage over the few pixels where a branch leaves its track. At 2× the corpus comparison finds no pixel it cannot explain. | Rune paints each stroke on its own and has no union of shapes. Measured on railroad's corpus: `hairlines.txt` lists the pixels. |
| Icons | The page's host draws the named icon in its box, from what its `icons` resolver answers for the name, in `currentColor`, which the root sets to the foreground. Where it answers nothing, or there is no host, the box shows Pele's built-in glyph for the name, or else stays empty. Architecture knows `database`, `server`, `disk`, `internet` and `cloud`. Treeview puts its folder or its file in every icon box it has no icon for. A label's `fa:` icon, a mindmap node's and a kanban card's get room only where the host has the icon. | The app's icons, from `ViewOptions::icons`, in the boxes of architecture's services and groups, treeview's rows, flowchart's icon shapes and usecase's icon actors: fitted as SVG fits a view box, `currentColor` in the palette's foreground, an icon's own colours as they are. `pele render` and `pele parity` give Pele no icons, so the view can draw an icon where Pele's SVG, and a page with no icons, leave the box empty. Where the app has no icon for a name, the built-in glyph as Pele's SVG draws it, or an empty box. Every diagram is laid out as for a host with no icons, so a label's `fa:` icon, a mindmap node's and a kanban card's get no room and are not drawn. An icon that reaches outside its box is drawn whole, where SVG cuts it at the box, and one with a fill whose contours cross each other is not drawn. | The app supplies its icons, as Pele's host does. They are drawn when the drawing is painted, so another set lays nothing out, and room for a label's icon would lay the diagram out again. The lowering cuts no shape at a box, and does not work out what a fill whose contours cross covers. |
| Gradients | A `linearGradient` the page paints pixel by pixel. | Upright bands of one colour each, cut on device pixels: one to a pixel, or wider so that at most 128 lie along the gradient. Each band is the colour SVG has at its middle, so two neighbours are 2 levels apart at the most. | Rune fills a polygon in one colour, and runs a gradient only over an element's own box. Measured against resvg: flat colours within 1 level. |
| A sankey's links without an id prefix | One colour each, the colour of the node a link leaves. Pele fills links along gradients only under an `idPrefix` of letters, digits, `_` and `-`, so that two SVGs in one page never share a gradient's id. `render` has none unless given one; `mount` always gives one, so in a page the links are gradients. | Gradients: a view lays out under `pele`, Pele's own default, when the app gives no prefix. A prefix the app gives still decides, as in Pele. | A view declares no ids, so no two drawings can share one. A cynefin's boundaries, which Pele seeds from the prefix or else from `pele`, are the same. |
| Fills a device pixel deep | A browser's or resvg's coverage of the fill, by the area of each pixel it covers. | Rune's coverage, by each pixel's distance from the nearest side of the fill in the triangle it lies in. A sankey link one point deep at one device pixel a point is bolder where it runs nearly level. Everything at 2× is the same. | Rune's renderer. Measured on one corpus sankey of 68 links, which `side-by-side.txt` lists at 1×. How much bolder is read from Rune's shader, not measured: up to half of full coverage. |
| Where a link is pressed | Anywhere on the shapes and text inside the link. | Anywhere in the box around them. | Rune hit-tests an element's box. |
| Where a shape's tooltip shows | Anywhere on the shape that holds it, as a sankey link holds its own. | Anywhere in a row of boxes 16 points wide along the shape. Where the boxes of two shapes overlap, the shape drawn last shows its tooltip. | Rune hit-tests an element's box. Measured on a sankey of 68 links: nine points in ten on a link show that link's tooltip. |
| Tooltips | The browser's, from the SVG's `<title>`. | Rune's `help` tooltip in the system's look, after the system's hover delay. | Rune's own tooltip. |
| Fitting the width there is | `mount` lays a diagram that draws itself narrower out again at the element's exact width, and the SVG's `max-width:100%` shrinks whatever is still too wide, however small its text gets. | `ViewOptions::fit` lays out again only at steps a twelfth of an octave apart, at the step under the width, so a diagram that narrows may leave up to 5.6% of the width unused. It shrinks what is still too wide until plain text is 11 points (settable), and scrolls sideways beyond that. It never draws a diagram larger than its size. | Dragging a window's edge zooms between steps instead of laying out every frame, and text stays readable on a phone. |
| Turning a diagram that runs across | `mount` draws a flowchart, or another of the 14 types that run across or down, running down only when the element is under 640 points wide (`directionBreakpoint`) and across does not fit. Wider, across is drawn and `max-width:100%` shrinks it. | `ViewOptions::fit` also turns it at any width where across, drawn at the smallest text size, would still be wider than the step it is laid out at, so that the view would scroll. Outside a fit, Pele's 640 points decide. The types are flowchart, state, class, er, requirement, usecase, packet, timeline, git, journey, agentflow, ishikawa, mindmap and eventmodeling. | Text stays readable without scrolling sideways, on a phone or in a column of a chat. The fit decides at its step, so it may turn up to 5.6% of the width before across would scroll. Measured on `across.mmd`: it turns at 797 points, where across it scrolled from about 780 down to 640. |
| Plain text a narrower layout sets smaller | `mount` lays a diagram out again at the element's width however small that layout sets its text, as a treemap does in small cells, and `max-width:100%` shrinks it further. | A fitted view uses a narrower layout only where the text the diagram draws at the plain size or larger at its own size can still be drawn at the smallest text size. Elsewhere it shrinks the diagram's own layout to that size and scrolls. | The smallest text size holds for the text drawn. Measured on `treemap.mmd`: laid out narrower down to 280 points, and from 270 its own layout at zoom 0.6875, scrolling, where the narrower one drew `Events` at 9 points. |
| Following a link | The browser follows `href` into `target`. | The view opens nothing. The app's handler is told the link, its target and whether it names a note of the host. | The app decides where a link may lead. |
| A diagram and its links to assistive technology | The root is a `graphics-document` that names its type, labelled by its `<title>` and described by its `<desc>`. A link holds its tooltip as a `<title>`, which names it as SVG names a link; one with no tooltip is named by the text inside it. | The drawing is a figure labelled by `accTitle` and described by `accDescr`. A link is named by the text it shows, or its address where it shows none, and its tooltip describes it. Tab reaches each link and Return follows it. | Rune has no role for a graphics document. A link reads as the label people see. |

Where a browser and resvg draw Pele's SVG differently, the view follows the browser, Pele's host. resvg is only the reference the tests draw Pele's SVG with. A fill or stroke that is no colour, such as `fill:#0` or `fill:blu`, is one such case. A page's CSS drops the declaration, and the element keeps the colour it has without it. resvg paints such a fill black and draws no such stroke. The view drops it as a page does, and draws the diagram natively.

## While a source fails

As Pele's `mount` does, a source that fails after one that drew leaves that drawing shown: by default at 0.4 opacity, as one layer, with the message's first and last lines over its bottom edge and the whole message as their tooltip. `Diagram::status()` is then `RenderStatus::Kept`, and `Diagram::error()` says why. `KeptLook::Unchanged` shows the drawing as it was, for a source still streaming in. As `mount` draws its last parsed text again for a new width or fonts, the view draws the last source that drew as its options, width and device scale draw it now: in a fitted view it follows the fit's steps and zoom. The failing source is never laid out for it.

| Area | Pele's `mount` | rune-pele product mode | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A failure with nothing drawn yet | The whole message in the element, in monospace, in the error colour. | The view is the error, and the app shows it. | As the view was before it kept drawings: an app decides how to show an error. |
| A change of options while the source fails | `update` leaves the drawing as it is. The last text that parsed is drawn with the new options at the next change of width or fonts. | The last source that drew is drawn with the view's new options at once, as for a new width, fit step, zoom, font or device scale. | A Rune view is built from the app's state each frame, so there is no drawing to leave as it was. |
| The error's colour | `--pele-error`, else `#e45756`. | The palette's critical colour: Pele's `#e45756` in `Palette::pele()` and in the window's Rune theme. | The palette has no error role, and Pele's two fallbacks are the same red. |
| The error's text | 0.8 of the host element's font size, in its font. | 0.8 of the view's font size, 16 unless set, in the app's sans font. | The view's font size stands in for the element's, which Rune has no notion of. |
| The error's width | The element's, cut with an ellipsis. | The drawing's, or the parent's where the parent stretches the view across, as a fitted view's frame does, cut with an ellipsis. | The view is no block: it is as wide as its drawing unless its parent stretches it. |

## What is drawn by rune-mermaid instead

`Diagram::status()` is then `RenderStatus::RuneMermaid`, and `Diagram::fallback()` says why. Where rune-mermaid cannot draw the source either, the view keeps the last drawing that drew, as the next section says, or with none is an error and draws nothing.

rune-mermaid's drawing does less than Pele's, which draws every source it parses. It has no links, so a press tells the app nothing, and no tooltips. Of the view's options it follows only the width and the kept look. It ignores `ViewOptions::zoom`, the palette, the font size and the app's icons, and draws its own 13 point text in Rune's neutral colours. Wider than the view's width, or than a fitted view's, it shrinks to 0.6 of its size whatever its text then measures, and past that shows its middle with its sides faded out. `Diagram::engine()` says who drew the drawing a view shows, kept or not, and `Engine::has_links`, `Engine::has_tooltips` and `Engine::zooms` say what it can do.

| Source | Why it is not drawn natively |
| --- | --- |
| A diagram type pele-core has not ported | There is no Scene to draw. |
| A style declaration the Scene's walk does not read: a colour only a page can resolve (`var()`, `color-mix()`, `currentcolor`), a font family other than `monospace`, `text-decoration` other than `underline`, a letter or word spacing, a dash offset, a line cap or join other than butt, round and miter, a `ry` unlike `rx`, or a font size not in pixels | Pele writes the declaration into the SVG for the page to apply. Read without a page, the drawing would differ from Pele's without saying so. |
| A node with a picture | The picture is the host's to load, and the view loads nothing. |
| A fill along a gradient that does not run level in the drawing, or that fills a shape with a hole | A gradient is drawn as upright bands, which neither can be cut into. Pele's only gradients are a sankey's links, which run level and have no holes. |
| A fill whose contours cross each other | What it fills depends on where they cross, which the lowering does not work out. A contour that crosses only itself, as an hourglass does, is drawn. No corpus or harvested source has such a fill. |
