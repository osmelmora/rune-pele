# Native Rune view

A Rune app calls `rune_pele::Diagram::view` with Mermaid source and gets the diagram as native Rune elements: the same Scene the SVG writer reads, laid out with Rune's own text measurement and drawn as keyed shapes and text in Pele's paint order. All 33 types are drawn this way: the kinds `rune_pele::lower::NATIVE` lists. A palette or dark-mode change repaints without parsing, laying out or measuring again. `Diagram::view_with` tells the app when a link is pressed, and a tooltip shows where the pointer rests. Any other type, and a source whose Scene asks for something the lowering does not draw, is drawn by Rune's own `rune-mermaid`; any other failure comes back as an error. A source that fails after one that drew, as one still streaming in does, keeps that drawing on screen instead: dimmed with the error over its bottom edge as Pele's `mount` shows it, or unchanged with `KeptLook::Unchanged`. `ViewOptions::fit` fits a diagram to the width it has, and a kept drawing follows that fit.

## Sub-features

- `rune-snap` renders an example headless with Rune's `--snap`, in the system's light or dark theme: the `pie` example, and the `diagram` example for any Mermaid file.
- `rune-palette` switches the pie example's palette with its button, by a scripted tap or the `hues` scene.
- `rune-product` checks pie-pets through the public API at 1× and 2×: each slice's area against the Scene's angles and radius, ink in every label's box, a palette change with no layout, keys that survive an `idPrefix` and a value edit, a whole donut, an empty pie, and the `rune-mermaid` fallback.
- `rune-side-by-side` rasterises Pele's own SVG with resvg beside the native PNG and compares the pie's slice areas.
- `rune-graph` draws every corpus source of the 31 types it opens (every type `NATIVE` lists but pie) natively at 1× and 2× beside Pele's SVG through resvg, and holds each to three limits over the pixels that are not text.
- `rune-graph-product` checks one fixture of each opened type: a key per drawing that survives an `idPrefix` and a value edit, a palette change with no layout, the fallback and its reason, a link's event and a tooltip of every type whose Scene has them, a casing the pointer passes through, and a gantt chart's today marker set by `ViewOptions::now`.
- `rune-hairlines` sorts every pixel that tells a 1× drawing of a standalone type from Pele's by the stroke it lies on in the Scene's geometry: slanted or curved, level or upright, or none.
- `rune-scene-features` counts, over the corpus and every harvested source of the standalone types, why the lowering refuses a source and each feature it draws only approximately, and marks what the corpus never shows.
- `rune-lowering` checks each lowering on its own against analytic geometry and resvg: dashes, arcs, holes, translucent strokes, corners and ends, boxes, a turned shape, text anchors, weights, turned text, the outline behind a title, a group's opacity, an icon's built-in glyph, a group that moves and turns, a turned axis title against Pele's, and a sankey's gradients under the default id prefix.
- `rune-break` breaks fourteen lowerings, five parts of the kept drawing and eight parts of zoom and fit and four parts of a kept drawing's fit one at a time, then three parts of a view's identity and two of a fit's text and turning, seven parts of what a page shows, six of what a page drops and twelve of the app's icons, and shows the feature test of each fail.
- `rune-keep` keeps the last drawing that drew while a source fails: every character prefix of every fixture through one `Diagram`, with no prefix of a fixture that draws natively left to rune-mermaid, the dimmed look against the last drawing at Pele's opacity, the unchanged look pixel for pixel, a kept rune-mermaid drawing, a palette or dark-mode change with no layout, options refused, and a source that draws again. The `diagram` example streams a file in with `--stream`.
- `rune-kept-fit` keeps the last drawing in a fitted view: while a source fails and the width goes from 900 points to 300, each frame in either look is the last good source as a view of it draws at that width, from the one frame the fit reads its width from, and sources that lay out and then fail do not lay the kept drawing out again.
- `rune-gradient` draws sankey natively: its corpus sources beside Pele's SVG under the id prefix `pele`, which the view lays out under when the app gives none and under which Pele fills links along gradients, a palette change that recolours a gradient, and each link's tooltip where the pointer rests on it.
- `rune-census` counts what the Scenes of the graph types ask an interpreter to draw, over the corpus and every harvested source.
- `rune-perf` times layout, lowering and painting for the largest harvested source of each type and for stress cases.
- `rune-scene` checks the Scene's read model the Rune view is built on: paint order, keys, inherited style and palette resolution.
- `rune-fit` draws a diagram smaller in the lowering and fits a view to the width it has. It draws every corpus source of the 32 graph types at zoom 0.75 and 0.5 in a 2x window beside Pele's SVG at the same density, and each fixture at the spike's zooms both that way and with Rune's `El::scale`. It holds text at each zoom to its zoomed box and a stroke to its zoomed width. It walks a fit through its steps for a flowchart that turns, a pie whose plot narrows and an er diagram that cannot narrow, resizes from 900 points to 300, and presses a link and rests on tooltips at half size.
- `rune-fit-text` fits every fixture in Rune's headless runtime as its box narrows from 900 points to 200, and holds the text each draws at the plain size or larger at its own size to the smallest text size as drawn, after a narrower layout and the zoom. It also turns a flowchart that runs across wherever across would scroll at that size.
- `rune-identity` checks what a view is in its window: a `Diagram` that replaces another under the same id draws and links its own source, a fitted view reads its own frame's width beside a diagram whose id is its own with `~fit` after it, and Pele's metrics leave a gantt chart's today at the system clock.
- `rune-theme` checks the series colours a source sets in its theme config, at a pixel of git's main branch label: the source's in `Palette::pele()`, and the palette's own in any other. It also checks how the side-by-side reads those colours from Pele's SVG, and a git commit's tooltip.
- `rune-engine` checks that `Diagram::engine()` says what the drawing shown does: a link pressed, a tooltip where the pointer rests, and a zoom, natively, by rune-mermaid, and kept.
- `rune-page-rules` draws what a page shows of Pele's SVG where the SVG's own rules decide: a rectangle, circle or ellipse of no size draws nothing, a declaration whose value its property does not take, such as a fill that is no colour or a width still being typed, is dropped as a page's CSS drops it and leaves the diagram native, runs of spaces show as SVG's `xml:space` shows them, and the diagram's name and description and each link reach assistive technology and the keyboard.
- `rune-icons` draws the app's icons, given as `ViewOptions::icons`, in the icon boxes of architecture, flowchart, treeview and usecase: each beside Pele's own SVG with the same icons as markup in its boxes, at 2×, at zoom 0.75 and 0.5 and in a fit; a name the app has no icon for as without icons; another function painted again with no layout; a link and a tooltip found on an icon; and SVG path data read as SVG reads it. The `diagram` example gives three icons, and `--no-icons` leaves the boxes as Pele's SVG does for a page with none.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- An app calls `Diagram::new(id, FontCatalog::…)` once and `diagram.view(cx, source, ViewOptions::new(width))` in its view. `diagram.view_with(…, on_event)` also passes a plain function that hears `Event::Activate(link)`.
- The drawing is a figure that assistive technology names by the source's `accTitle` and describes by its `accDescr`, which `Scene::accessible_title` and `Scene::accessible_description` give. Under `view_with`, each link is a Tab stop named by the label it shows, and Return follows it as a press does.
- `Diagram::status()` says who drew the last view, and `Diagram::fallback()` why it was left to rune-mermaid. While a source fails after one that drew, `status()` is `RenderStatus::Kept`, `Diagram::error()` says why the source fails, and the view shows the last drawing that drew. `ViewOptions::kept_look(KeptLook::Unchanged)` shows it as it was, with no error over it, for an app that streams a source in and shows the error itself, or not at all. While a source streams in, a style value half typed, such as `stroke-width:2p`, `opacity:0.` or `font-weight:bol`, is dropped as a page's CSS drops it, so the frame is still drawn natively. Only a value a page applies and the walk does not draw, such as `text-decoration:line-through`, leaves it to rune-mermaid. The drawing kept is the last source that drew, drawn as the view's options and width draw it now: a fitted view turns or shrinks it as the width changes.
- `cargo run -p rune-pele --example diagram -- <file.mmd> --stream` types the file in, three characters a frame. Add `--kept-unchanged` for the unchanged look. The line above the diagram counts the characters that have arrived and, while a drawing is kept, gives the error's first line.
- `cargo run -p rune-pele --example pie` opens the pie window. Its button reads `Palette: Pele` or `Palette: hues`.
- `cargo run -p rune-pele --example diagram -- <file.mmd>` opens any Mermaid file. The line above the diagram says who drew it, and names the last link pressed.
- Both examples take Rune's tooling flags: `--snap <png>`, `--theme light|dark`, `--script <json>`, `--size WxH`, `--scale N`. The pie example also takes `--scene hues`.
- A crate user reads the Scene through `pele_core::Scene::paint`, `Scene::resolve_paints` and `Scene::unread_styles`. `cargo run -p rune-pele --example walk -- <file.mmd>` prints that walk.
- `ViewOptions::fit()` fits the diagram to the width of the view's parent, which the view reads from Rune's layout: its own size where that fits, laid out again for the width where the type draws itself narrower, smaller down to 11 point plain text (`ViewOptions::smallest_text`), then scrolling sideways. Plain text is what the diagram draws at the view's font size or larger at its own size, and the 11 points hold for it as drawn: a narrower layout that sets it smaller is not used. A diagram that runs across and can run down, as a flowchart can, runs down wherever across would scroll at 11 points, at any width. The fit's frame is keyed with the diagram's id, around the drawing keyed the same. `ViewOptions::zoom(z)` draws it `z` times its size, after any fit. `Diagram::zoom()` says the zoom the last view drew at, and `ViewStats::lowerings` counts lowerings, once for each layout and zoom.
- The `diagram` example fits its window. Its first line says the width the diagram is laid out at and the zoom it is shown at, so `--size` shows each step.
- A gantt chart marks today at `ViewOptions::now(milliseconds)`. Without it, the chart takes the system clock when the view lays it out and keeps that time: the marker moves only when the view lays the chart out again for another reason, such as a new source, width or font epoch. An app that wants the marker to follow the day passes `now` itself, for example the start of the current day. Each new value lays the diagram out again, and the same value does not.
- `Diagram::engine()` says who drew the drawing the last view showed, whether its source drew it or the view kept it: `Engine::Pele`, or `Engine::RuneMermaid`, whose drawing has no links or tooltips and ignores the zoom. `Engine::has_links`, `has_tooltips` and `zooms` say so, and `None` means nothing was shown.

## Gotchas

- resvg paints a fill it cannot parse black and draws no stroke it cannot parse: `style A fill:#0` gives a black node. A page's CSS drops such a declaration, and the node keeps its paint. The walk drops it as a page does, so `invalid_colour` holds the drawing to resvg's drawing of Pele's SVG with that declaration taken out. The same holds for every property the walk meets, by the grammars in `crates/pele-core/src/scene/css.rs`: `stroke-width:2p`, `opacity:0.` or `font-weight:bol` is dropped, and `invalid_value` takes it out of Pele's SVG before resvg draws it. A value that leaves a function open, as `fill:rgb(1 2 3` in a state diagram's `classDef`, takes in the declarations after it on a page, so the walk drops them all. Where browsers disagree, a value counts as applied if one of them applies it: a number with no unit is a length to every property.

- A kept drawing is the last source that drew, drawn again as the view's options, width and device scale draw it now. While none of them changes nothing is laid out or lowered again, even after the layout cache has dropped it. A new zoom lowers it again, and a new step of a fit, font size, font or device scale lays it out again. The failing source is tried, as every frame tries its source, and is never laid out for the kept drawing. A palette or dark-mode change repaints it. A kept rune-mermaid drawing is drawn again by rune-mermaid for a dark-mode change or a new width, since rune-mermaid has no colours to change otherwise.
- The dimmed look puts the drawing inside a wrapper keyed as the diagram, so Rune makes its elements anew when a drawing is kept and again when a source draws, as it did when the view was an error. `KeptLook::Unchanged` shows the very elements of the last drawing, so nothing is made anew. In a fitted view the wrapper sits inside the frame the fit reads its width from, keyed with the id too, so the width does not change with the look. Rune finds the frame as the first element keyed with the id.
- In the dimmed look the error line is as wide as the view: the drawing's width, or the parent's where the parent stretches the view across, as a fitted view's frame does. Its text is cut with an ellipsis, and its tooltip holds the whole message.

- Pele fills a sankey link along a gradient only where the drawing has an id prefix of letters, digits, `_` and `-`. A view lays out under `pele` when the app gives none, and the side-by-side draws Pele's SVG under `pele` too, so both fill links along gradients. Pele's `render` with no `idPrefix`, as `pele parity` draws the corpus, fills every link in one colour. That is Pele's own drawing, not a missing gradient.

- Text on a series colour contrasts with that colour in the Rune view, by Pele's own luminance rule. Pele's SVG does so only where the source sets the series colour, so its `17%` on orange is white. That difference is intended. It is not a parity failure; parity is the SVG writer's, under `parity.md`. `DIVERGENCES.md` lists every such difference.
- resvg has no CSS variables, and reads neither the root's `font` shorthand nor a corner radius in a `style`. The side-by-side resolves each `var()` as a page with no CSS of its own would, sets the root's `font-size` from that shorthand, and moves `rx` from a `style` to the attribute. A raw `resvg` of Pele's SVG draws black shapes with square corners and a 12px title.
- A source's theme config can set its series colours (`git0`, `cScale0`, `pie1`, `xyChart.plotColorPalette`). Pele declares them on the SVG's root as `--pele-series-N`, and the side-by-side reads a variable the root declares as a page does, not its fallback. The Rune view draws them only in `Palette::pele()`. Any other palette wins over them, as `DIVERGENCES.md` says, so such a source in another palette differs from Pele's SVG by design.
- The side-by-side needs Arial or a metric-compatible face: Arial on macOS or Windows, or Liberation Sans on Linux. `RUNE_PELE_RESVG_FONT=<face.ttf>` names another face, such as the Inter Rune draws with.
- Without `RUNE_PELE_OUT`, the pie test writes to `out/verify/pie-rune/`, the graph tests and the lowering test to `out/verify/rune-graph/`, and the spike tests to `out/verify/rune-spike/`.
- An app's view lays out with Inter's widths, so a node is as wide as its Inter label. The graph side-by-side lays out with Pele's own metrics instead (`ViewOptions::pele_metrics`), so that every shape is where Pele's SVG has it, and leaves text out of the comparison. Text drawn in Inter then does not fit its Arial-sized box exactly. Never judge label fit from a side-by-side image.
- At 1× a slanted or curved 1 point stroke is drawn a little darker by Rune than by resvg, all along it. The first two limits are therefore held at 2× and only listed at 1×, where a drawing is held to a patch of at most 30 px. A 2× row over a limit is a defect.
- The corpus side-by-side is ignored in a debug build, where its 860 comparisons take many minutes, so `mise run check` does not run it. Run it with `--release`, as above. A run that reports `7 passed; 0 failed; 2 ignored` was a debug run and compared nothing.
- At 1× resvg draws a stroke one device pixel wide or thinner as a hairline, with no joins: a 1 point mitred corner has no tip there. At 2×, or at width 1.01, it draws the tip. The Rune view draws it at every scale, as SVG says. And resvg covers a pixel in quarters across, so an upright 1.5 point line at x = 681.88 is two equal greys there and 0.87 and 0.63 of the colour here.
- `break-lowering.sh` edits source files and puts them back. It touches each file as it does, because cargo goes by modification times and would otherwise keep the binary of the broken file. Do not interrupt it; if it is interrupted, `git checkout crates/rune-pele/src crates/pele-core/src` restores the sources.
- Without `ViewOptions::icons`, an icon's box shows Pele's built-in glyph where Pele's SVG has one: architecture's `database`, `server`, `disk`, `internet` and `cloud`, and treeview's folder and file in every treeview icon box. Any other box is left empty, as Pele's SVG leaves it for its host. A flowchart with `fa:` icons shows no icons, with the app's icons or without: Pele leaves a label room for an icon only where its host has the icon, and the view lays out as for a host with none.
- resvg reads no declaration of the root's style that follows a custom property, so it draws `currentColor` black where a page draws Pele's foreground: the root's `color:var(--_fg)` comes after `--_fg`. `icons` gives the root the colour as an attribute too before resvg draws Pele's SVG with icons.
- At 1×, resvg samples a pixel's coverage on a quarter-pixel grid (tiny-skia's `SUPERSAMPLE_SHIFT = 2`). An architecture group's icon is 20 points across, so its glyph's level edges fall on eighths of a pixel, and resvg moves them an eighth. Rune covers them exactly. So the cloud's flat base shows a short red run at 1×, with the rows at 50% and 75% coverage in resvg against 62.5% and 62.5% here. Every edge falls on quarter pixels at 2×, which passes.
- zsh passes an unquoted `${x:+--scene $x}` as one word, and Rune ignores an argument it does not know. Write `--scene hues` as two literal words.
- A tap at `t: 0.1` needs a frame laid out first. The script runner provides one, so the tap finds the `palette` key.
